Painting requirements

Preface

The painting requirement described in this document comes from the "Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva Recitation Ritual" recorded in a scripture collection, Japanese named "Keiran Shūyō Shū". The original text can be viewed here.

Japanese in the past brought Chinese translated scriptures from China to Japan. So the deities' descriptions are written in classical Chinese, I try my best to translate them to English. The reference images are to give you a brief idea on what deities look like. You are free to apply your personal drawing style, like Paubha style.

Also different artists may have different religious backgrounds. So deities described here may have conflicts with yours. If you strongly believe that some descriptions are wrong or in doubt, please raise me questions for clarification.

The finished jobs will be posted to this site for people to appreciate. My target auidences would be Hongkongers and you may see this opportunity as a showcase to let more people know more about your Paubha art works. I could give you a credit by including your contact information like instagram or facebook page along with the finished jobs.

Deities' descriptions

Yama

Original text:
「一蓮花座ノ下ノ左ノ方ニ畫閻魔使者。身白肉色左手ニ持寶鏡。右ノ手ニ執檀荼幢」

Translation:
  1. His skin tone is white in color.
  2. His left hand is holding a Treasure mirror.
  3. His right hand is holding a Sceptre of human heads.

Expectation:
Yama is a God of death and justice in hell realm. Just like a judge, he should be middle aged and look cool and serious. In Japan, Yama looks like a celestial being:
You may take Svacchanda Bhairava as reference:
Please note that the role of Yama here is acting as a judge, not a punisher. So it is not necessary to draw too much scary outfits like necklace made of human heads, animals' parts etc. on him. I think you may preserve the crown decorated with skulls to show him somehow related to death.

You may also draw him sitting on a buffalo with the right knee bent while the left leg hangs down.

Strongest Tamer

Original text:
「三次畫大力使者。身青黒色載師子王左手持獸稍。右手開赦押右ノ腰。以忿怒惡眼觀四方形」

  1. His skin tone is blue-black in color.
  2. A Lion king as his vechicle.
  3. His left hand holds a Whip to tame the beasts.
  4. His right hand placing on the waist and the elbow turned outward.
  5. Look around with a very angry face.

Expectation:
He should look like a kind of Asura with a strong body shape. To make him look terrifying, facial features should include three eyes and sharp teeth. His whole body should be on fire. The Lion king he is riding on is also roaring angry. His role is to tame beasts belong to animal realm, not to kill them. So no humans' or animals' parts outfits appeared on him.

Goddess of Treasure (Tāraka)

Original text:
「五次畫寶藏天女身。齊色七寶衣甲。以惡眼視一切鬼神形。左手持舍利塔。右ノ手執寶棒」

  1. She wears a crown, earrings, necklaces, bracelets, a waist belt etc., a full body of jewellery, made of gold and colorful gemstones.
  2. Her left hand holding a Stupa.
  3. Her right hand holding a lightweight Gada as a weapon.
  4. Role as a dharma protector, angry staring at all kinds of evil spirits.

Expectation:
Esoteric Buddhism in Japan believes that, she is a incarnation of Aryavalokiteshvara like Tara with great power. In some ritals, she is crossover with Vaiśravaṇa (known as Kubera) and appeared as a Deva. In summary, she is a Queen or King of Yakṣa with great power and holding treasures. Her divine form looks like a young lady with an adorable face and smooth skin, even her breath smells good. Her signature features are wearing purple clothes and head-to-toe jewellery made of colored gemstones and gold.
As Vasudhārā also know as "Gold Tara", I strongly believe that they belong to the same deity with strong connection to Aryavalokiteshvara. You may take Vasudhārā as reference and change the features accordingly:
If she is a Queen of Yakṣa, then she should be a Yakṣiṇī. A Yakṣiṇī symbolized fertility, so she should has an excellent feminine body as well.
Her role is a protector in human realm. So she has a sharp eyes, watching all kinds of spirits not to do evil things and maintain dharma.

Scene

  • Six deities are separately drawn on six paintings.
  • The scene is similar to Vasudhārā painting.
  • In the center of lotus pool, there is a large leaf growing above the water surface, supporting the deity.
  • Some deities may not have their beasts to mount on, in this case, you may draw a stage on the top of the leaf for them to stand or sit.
  • If you think there is too much workload for you, for the back of each deity, you may consider avoid drawing complex structure. A simple circle of aureole or fire is also fine.
  • Other drawing details like sky or landscape are up to you.
  • Please bear in mind that the propotion of these six deities. Deva will be physically larger than Devi and childlike-god will be shorter than other deities. One option is to exaggerate the backgrounds to make the childlike-god looks "small" like the fairy painting.

Canvas size

  • Each canvas size around A6 size (105 x 148 millimeters).
  • Portrait orientation.
  • Acrylic paint will be fine.
